# Locant: freeze the screen while you point

**Status:** Spec written Oct 26, 2026, after Malik could not capture a window that opens from the menu bar. His capture of Monister's window (a SwiftUI `MenuBarExtra` in `.window` style) named it (`window`, 360×468) but its image was wallpaper. Measured the same day with the installed 1.0.1: that window stays open through ⌃⌃, the overlay, and the overlay taking key focus, and closes within 0.3 s of the click. Point takes its crop after the click, so the window is gone by then. A standard menu (`NSMenu`; Cursor's status menu was tested) closes as soon as the overlay becomes key, before any hover. While one is open it takes every mouse event, so an overlay that is not key gets no hover and no click (a probe panel counted 0 moves). A click on the ball closes either one before Point opens. Decided the same day: the screen freezes when an action starts, as Malik asked. Numbered from R119 (PR #72 holds R116–R118).
**Amended Nov 13, 2026 (R119, R173):** Malik reported that Snap still did not freeze what he was looking at: what an app shows on hover was gone from the picture, often as soon as he moved the mouse. R119 put the overlay up first and captured the screen after. Measured that day with a probe window that shows a card only while the mouse is over it: with the mouse held still, the probe lost its hover 120 ms after the hotkey, because the overlay had come up under the cursor and taken the mouse, and the capture came 100 to 200 ms after that. The order is now the other way round: the capture first, the overlay after (R173). R119 is rewritten to match.
**Amended Nov 13, 2026 (R174):** with R173 the picture kept what showed on hover, but the hover still read the app as it is now. Over a hover card that has closed, the outline and the Option levels came from what is behind it. Malik asked whether that could be fixed. It can: the tree near the pointer is read before the overlay opens too, and it answers for what the app has dropped since (R174). The two requirements of Nov 13 are numbered R173 and R174: `specs/source-hints.md` holds R163–R172, and the first of them was written as R169 before that work merged.
**Scope of this spec:** the freeze for Point, Snap, Text, and Cut; crops from it; a window that closed since the freeze; the ball while a menu is open.
**Out of scope:** Color (its magnifier stays live); the items inside a standard menu (it is closed before the hover can read them); iterations (R52), which stay live.

This spec overrides `specs/v0.1.md` R2's "No frozen frame". R2 counted on the front app keeping focus to keep menus open. A standard menu closes anyway, and a window-style one closes at the click.

## 1. Requirements

**R119. The freeze.** When Point, Snap, Text, or Cut opens, from any entry (a hotkey, the ball, the menu bar, the ring, Try it), Locant first captures every display at full resolution, its own windows excluded (R173). Then the overlay panels come up, each with its display's image already under the dim, and the first takes key focus at once. From then on the overlay shows the screen as it was: a menu that closes, a video that plays on, or a window that moves underneath shows only once the overlay closes. The dim fades in as before, over the same picture, so nothing flickers. The images are released when the session ends. Without Screen Recording permission, or when the capture fails or has not answered within 1 s, the overlay opens over the live screen, as before this spec.

**R120. Crops from the freeze.** Point (an element, a set, a drawn frame), Snap, Text, and Cut cut their image from the frozen image of the display that holds the crop's center, with the same pixel math as a live crop: `ScreenCapture.crop(_:from:display:scale:)`, pure. With no frozen image of that display, the crop is taken live as before, and a stuck capture service is handled as in R110.

**R121. A window that closed.** The window list is read when the action starts (R3). When the frontmost window under the cursor in that list (any layer, Locant's own skipped) is no longer on screen, the hover does not read what is now behind it. The selection is that window: role `window`, its app's name as the label, its bounds as the frame, no Option levels. A click captures it from the frozen image, with that app as the source and no window title. Snap, Text, and Cut take the same selection; a frame drawn from a point on such a window keeps no elements. The test is pure: `Geometry.vanishedWindow(at:windows:excludingPID:isOnScreen:)`. A window that is still open, as Monister's is until the click, resolves as before.

**R122. The ball while a menu is open.** Another app has a menu open when it has an on-screen window from the pop-up menu level (101) up to, not including, the screen saver level (1000). Status items (25) and the transparent panels some apps keep at 1000 (Wispr Flow) do not count. Pure: `Geometry.hasOpenMenu(windows:excludingPID:)`. While the ball is awake or ready, Locant keeps a session event tap on the left mouse button. A press on the disc while a menu is open is taken by Locant before any app sees it, so the menu stays open, and its release starts Point, which then freezes the menu (R119). That press neither opens the ring nor drags the ball. Every other press, and every press while no menu is open, passes through untouched. Without the tap (no Accessibility trust) the ball behaves as before.

**R123. Words elsewhere.** README's limitation "Menus and popovers stay open under the overlay, but an element inside another app's menu may not resolve." says what R119 and R121 do instead. CHANGELOG gains one bullet under Unreleased.

**R173. The picture comes first.** Nothing of Locant's goes on screen, and Locant takes no key focus, until the capture of R119 has answered or its 1 s is up. The overlay takes the mouse from the app under the cursor the moment it comes up, and that app then drops what it showed on hover: a hover menu on a web page, a tooltip, a row's hover buttons. A standard menu closes once the overlay is key. So the capture is the session's first act, started before the front app is read (R5), and the overlay waits for it. The displays and windows are listed once for all displays (`ScreenCapture.freeze()`), not once per display. Until the overlay shows, the mouse and keys still belong to the app in front, as they did before the hotkey. What the user gives up is the dim appearing at once: it now appears when the picture has arrived. Measured Nov 13, 2026 on Malik's Mac with the built-in display and one 6K display, from the hotkey to the overlay on screen, five sessions each: 70 to 90 ms before this requirement (120 ms the first session after launch), 170 to 210 ms with it (360 ms the first).

**R174. The elements come first too.** While the capture of R119 runs, and before the overlay opens, Locant reads the accessibility tree of the app under the pointer: from the application down, nearest the pointer first, one message per node (its attributes, its page address on a web area, and its children together). A node more than 600 pt from the pointer is recorded, but what it holds is not. The walk stops at 1500 nodes, or 150 ms after the session began, whichever comes first, so the overlay waits for it no longer than it usually waits for the picture. It is skipped without Screen Recording permission, since only a frozen screen keeps showing what has gone.

While the screen is frozen, a recorded node counts as gone once it, or a node above it, is no longer among its parent's children: a view that was hidden, a web node that was unmounted or stopped being displayed, a window that closed. That is asked of the app as the pointer moves, a parent's children at most every 400 ms, and a node found gone stays gone. An answer read 2 s or more after the recording is kept for the session: by then the app has dropped what it showed on hover, so hover costs no more reads after that. Where the pointer is over a recorded node that is gone, the hover reads the recording instead of the app: the deepest such node whose frame holds the point (a node inside a window, scroll area, or web area that does not hold the point is left out, and so is a node whose window had another normal window in front of it at that point when the session began), refined toward a control as a live hit is (R2), with its ancestors, its neighbors, and its page address as recorded. The Option levels, a click, a Shift-click, Snap, Text, and Cut all follow from that snapshot as from a live one. This comes before R121, so an element recorded inside a window that closed is the selection, and the window as a whole remains the answer where nothing was recorded. A drawn frame lists the recorded elements it touches that are gone, and leaves out the live elements inside the frame of what went, which the picture shows covered. Everywhere else the app answers for itself, as before.

What this does not reach:
- **Beyond the walk.** A node that was not read in time, or sits under one more than 600 pt from the pointer, is read live as before.
- **A tree not built yet.** Chrome and Electron apps build theirs on first contact (R3), so in the first session over such an app the recording holds only the window.
- **Hidden but still listed.** A menu the page hides with `opacity: 0` and `pointer-events: none` alone stays in the app's tree, so it does not count as gone, and Chrome's live hit test answers with what is behind it (measured Nov 13, 2026). A menu hidden with `display: none`, or removed from the page, is gone and is read from the recording.
- **Device Hub's simulated screen** (R133): the recording does not carry that screen's special cases; nothing there shows on hover.
- **The source line of a web element** (`specs/web-source.md` R146) is not asked for an element read from the recording: the page would answer for what is at that point now.

Measured Nov 13, 2026 on Malik's Mac, one message per node against the apps that were open: Dia 297 nodes in 30 to 50 ms, Finder 122 in 100 ms, WeChat 66 in 6 ms, an Electron app 387 in 40 ms. One attribute per message took two to three times as long. From the hotkey to the overlay on screen, sessions two to five of two runs each, the pointer over an Electron app: 166 to 239 ms without the recording (median 189), 189 to 236 ms with it (median 212).

## 3. Tests

- `FrozenScreenTests`:
  - A crop from a 2× image of a display with a negative origin has the right point size, scale, crop rectangle, and pixels.
  - `FrozenScreen` crops from the display that holds the crop's center, and returns nil when none does.
- `TreeRecordingTests` (R174), on a page with a nav item, the menu that hangs under it, text behind the menu, and a row scrolled out of a list:
  - Hits come deepest first; the menu is hit outside its item's frame; the scrolled-out row is not hit beside its list.
  - A container gives way to a control within 8 pt, and stands when none is near.
  - A node did not show where another normal window, the app's own or another app's, lay in front of its window; a transparent panel at the screen saver level hides nothing.
  - A snapshot has the shape of a live read: full attributes on the element, light ones on ancestors and neighbors, DOM handles on a web node's path, the page address from the web area above.
  - A drawn frame keeps the recorded elements and drops the live ones under what went.
- `GeometryTests`:
  - `vanishedWindow` returns the frontmost window under the point when it has left the screen; nil when it is still there, when the point is on no window, and for Locant's own window.
  - `hasOpenMenu` is true for another app's window at 101 or 500, false for status items at 25, a normal window, another app's panel at 1000, and Locant's own windows.
- `FloatingBallTests`: the disc takes a press within its radius plus 2 pt of its center, and not in the window's clear margin.
- The existing tests pass.
- In the app:
  - **Monister's window**, open from the menu bar: ⌃⌃, click a control inside it. The window closes at the click, but the overlay still shows it, and the PNG shows the control, not wallpaper.
  - **A standard menu** (Cursor's status menu): ⌃⌃. The overlay still shows the menu; the hover over it reads `window "Cursor"`; the click's PNG is the menu.
  - **The ball with Monister's window open:** a click leaves the window open, and Point opens over it frozen.
  - **The ball with no menu open:** click, hold for the ring, and drag all work as before.
  - **Nothing else changes:** Point on a normal window, Snap, Text, Cut, a drawn frame, Shift sets, and Esc.
  - **An element of what shows on hover (R174):** the same window, its card now holding two buttons, with a third button behind the card. With the pointer on the card, Point's hotkey, then a click on a button of the card: the capture names that button, not the one behind. A frame drawn over the card lists the card's buttons and not the one behind.
  - **A hover menu on a web page (R174):** a page in Chrome with three nav items, each with a menu under it: one shown with `display`, one added to the page on enter and removed on leave, one shown with `opacity`. After one session over the page (Chrome builds its tree on first contact): the pointer on an item, Point's hotkey, a click on the menu's first link. The first two captures name the link's text with the path `.menu › .menu-link`; the third names the button behind the menu, the limit R174 states.
  - **What shows on hover (R173):** a window that shows a card only while the mouse is over it. With the mouse on it, Snap's hotkey, then the mouse held still or moved a few points: the overlay shows the card, though the window itself hid it when the overlay came up. Before R173 the overlay showed the window without the card, even with the mouse held still.
