**Scope of this spec:** finding shapes, hover and the ⌥ ladder with shapes in them, the cluster under any parent, a row of controls as a rung, Device Hub's parent links and floating bars, padded frames tightened in the phone, lines of text inside a merged element in the phone, the readout and payload of a shape, and the drawn frame that found nothing in Device Hub.

## 1. Requirements

**R124. Shapes, defined.** A shape is a region of the screen that reads as one container: one fill, bounded by visible edges, such as a pill, a capsule, a card, or a sheet. Locant finds shapes in the screen's pixels (R126, R127) and treats each one as an element:
- `role` `cluster`, `rawRole` `LocantShape`, `label` and `identifier` null, `identifierSource` `unknown`, `value` null.
- `frame`: the shape's bounds, in global CG points.
- `members`: the accessibility elements at least half inside the frame, in reading order (R129).
- `path`: the path of the innermost node, among the hovered element and its ancestors, whose frame contains the shape, then `cluster`.

The cluster computed from neighboring elements (v0.1 R2a rule 4, R128 here) keeps `rawRole` `LocantCluster`. The two differ only in `rawRole` and in the payload's wording (R129). A row of controls (R132) is a third, `rawRole` `LocantGroup`.

**R125. Hover lands on what is under the pointer.** For a pointer at point `p`, rung 0 of the ladder is chosen in this order:
1. **On an element.** The accessibility hit (after the reader's refinement) is a non-container and its frame contains `p`: that element.
2. **Holding inside a shape.** Step 3 finds a shape at `p`, rung 0 was an element inside that shape, and that element's frame grown by 3 pt contains `p`: the element stays. This replaces the 12 pt stickiness inside shapes. With no screen sample yet (R126) there is no shape, so no hold either, and step 4's 12 pt stickiness applies as today.
3. **In a shape.** `ShapeFinder` finds a shape at `p` (R127): that shape. A shape beats an element that the reader reached only through the 8 pt tolerance, whose frame does not contain `p`.
4. **Otherwise,** today's rules: the element reached through the 8 pt tolerance, the 12 pt stickiness, the cluster (R128), the container, or nothing (the fallback square).

Steps 2 and 3 apply only where the accessibility hit test answered at `p`. Where no app answers (iPhone Mirroring, a canvas), hover is unchanged.

The rest of the ladder runs from rung 0 outward:
- the row or column of controls that rung 0 belongs to, right after it, when there is one (R132);
- the shapes around rung 0 (R127, up to 3);
- the cluster (R128), only where there is no shape around rung 0;
- today's rungs: the accessibility ancestors and, in Snap and Cut, the window (R77).

**Order.** Rungs are ordered by containment: each one contains the one before it, give or take 2 pt. A rung that does not contain rung 0 is dropped, such as the leaf the reader reached through the 8 pt tolerance when rung 0 is a shape. A shape is placed after the last rung it contains and before the first rung that contains it. A shape that neither contains nor is contained by its neighbors on the ladder is dropped.

**Merging.** A shape whose frame matches an accessibility rung within 2 pt on every edge is merged with it, and the accessibility rung is kept because it carries a handle to grep. The 2 pt allows for pixel edges read at one sample per point. Two accessibility rungs still merge only within 1 pt (`collapsingSameFrames`, unchanged).

**Other modes.** Snap and Cut still open on the window rung, and ⌥ takes them to rung 0. Text takes the outline at its level, grown by the 8 pt tolerance, as today.

Haptics follow R59 unchanged: a move of the outline, into a shape or out of one, taps `.alignment`, and ⌥ taps `.levelChange`.

**R126. The screen sample.** `ScreenSampler` (new, `Locant/Capture/ScreenSampler.swift`, a class: it owns a capture task) keeps one recent image of the area hover reads shapes in, while the Point, Snap, Text, or Cut overlay is up for hover. Color does not use it. The area is R127's bound: the simulated screen in Device Hub, else the window under the pointer, else the display.

The image is a `PixelGrid`: RGBA, 8 bits per channel, top row first, at the display's own scale (2 samples per point on a Retina display). It carries the area it shows in global CG points, its scale, a generation number that increases with each new image, and R127's edge map. The conversion and the edge map are computed off the main actor when the image lands: about 27 ms for an iPhone screen, so hover never pays for them.

**Why the display's scale.** Measured on Oct 23 on Malik's Recent/Currents capsule, a glass edge is a one-pixel step of 8 to 10 levels at the display's scale. At one sample per point it splits into two steps of about 5, which an edge limit of 4 (R127) would barely catch. An area that would hold more than 4,000,000 samples is taken at a lower scale (`Geometry.sampleScale`), so a large browser window stays affordable.

- **Source:** `ScreenCapture.regionImage`, through the same filter as every capture, so Locant's own windows are never in it.
- **When:**
  - At once when hover wants an area other than the one the newest image shows, then every 1 s while the overlay stays up.
  - At most one capture is in flight.
  - When a new image lands, hover reads the spot under the pointer again.
  - The overlay takes the pointer's scrolling, so the screen under it changes only through animation and video.
- **Hover never waits for it.** Hover reads the newest image when it shows the area it wants. Otherwise `ShapeFinder` is not asked, and R125 step 4 decides.
- **Fails soft.** A failed or slow capture leaves the previous image, or none. An area whose capture failed is taken again at the next refresh, not each time hover wants it: a stuck capture service fails at once. No toast, no sound, no recovery alert. The click's own capture and its stuck-service handling (R109–R113) are unchanged.
- **Permission.** Without Screen Recording permission (`ScreenCapture.hasPermission()` false), the sampler never calls ScreenCaptureKit, so it can never cause a prompt, and hover runs on R125 step 4 alone.
- **Privacy.** The image lives in memory only, is never written, and is dropped as soon as hover ends: at a click, a drawn frame, or when the overlay closes. Shift-clicks into a set stay in hover.

**R127. Finding a shape.** `ShapeFinder` (new, `Locant/Resolve/ShapeFinder.swift`, pure) answers two questions from a `PixelGrid` and a bounding rectangle. The bound is the simulated screen in Device Hub (`HitRefiner.simulatedScreen`), else the window under the pointer, else the display.
- **The shape at a point** `p`. The seed color is the most common color in the 5×5 pt patch around `p`, so a pointer on an icon stroke seeds from the fill around it. Every patch sample within 4 of the seed color is a seed.
- **The shape around a rectangle** `r` (an element or a shape). The seed color is the most common color in a ring 2 pt outside `r`. Every ring sample within 4 of the seed color is a seed.

"Most common color": samples are counted after dropping the three low bits of each channel, and the seed color is the mean of the samples in the largest bucket. Distances in points are converted with the image's scale. A difference between two colors is the largest of the three channel differences, on a 0–255 scale.

**Edges first.**
- **An edge sample** is one whose color differs from one of its four neighbors by more than 4. Edges are mapped once per image (R126).
- **Both questions grow the same way:** from the seeds, a 4-connected fill takes each neighboring sample that is not an edge sample and is within 24 of the seed color. The fill never leaves the bound.
- **What that buys:**
  - A gradient of a level or two per sample is not an edge, so the fill crosses it.
  - A thin rim between two equal fills is an edge on both sides, so the fill stops before it. That is how a glass capsule is told from a background of its own color.
  - The seed limit stops a drift through a gap in an edge.
- **The background** is recognized as soon as the fill reaches two opposite sides of the bound, and the fill stops there.

**What was measured, Oct 23.** On the six real-screen fixtures (section 3), these rules find the Recent/Currents capsule, the toolbar capsule, a Claude message card, and a GitHub file row, and find no shape on any of the six backgrounds. The first rule, "within 6 of the sample it came from", leaked out of every glass shape at every tolerance tried: an antialiased corner ramps a few levels a sample.

They find neither:
- **the "Thought deleted" pill:** along most of its top edge, inside and outside match within 1 level and no rim is drawn;
- **the Notification sheet:** its bottom fades into the black margin over 8 samples without a step.

Neither has an edge on screen to find, so both fall back to the cluster (R128).

The result is the bounding box of the filled samples, accepted only if all of these hold. Otherwise there is no shape.
- It does not reach both the left and right edges of the bound, nor both the top and bottom (within 1 pt). A fill that touches two opposite sides is the background, not a shape. A sheet that meets only the bottom edge is still a shape.
- It covers less than 60% of the bound's area.
- It is at least 12 pt wide and 12 pt tall.
- For the shape around `r`: it contains `r` and is at least 2 pt larger than `r` on at least one side, so an element's own background is not its shape.

Enclosing shapes repeat the second question from the shape just found, up to 3 levels, and stop at the first that finds nothing.

`ShapeFinder` runs off the main actor. While the image's generation is unchanged and `p`'s sample lies in the last fill, the last answer is reused. The 4 and 24 were set by the real-screen fixtures on Oct 23; a change to either reruns them, and the spec is amended.

**R128. The cluster under any parent.** The reader reads the hovered element's siblings whenever its parent is a container, whatever the parent's size, with today's 400 ms cache and 60-child cap. When the hit is itself a container, its children are read whatever its size. A container under the pointer reads its children, not its siblings, so the sibling cluster that used to follow a small container hit is gone. v0.1 R2a rule 4 then applies under any parent: siblings group at a 24 pt gap, and the cluster's frame is the members' bounds plus 16 pt, kept inside the parent. As today, a cluster is added as a rung only when it covers less than 90% of its parent's area.

The cluster is the fallback for a shape:
- **At rung 0:** offered (R125 step 4) only when there is no shape at `p`.
- **After an element:** offered only when there is no shape around that element.

That covers no image yet, a fill that leaked or covered too much, and glass whose edges the fill could not hold.

**A container under the pointer** with no cluster around `p`:
- **It spans the window:** today's fallback square, `element: null` on click, and the container only through ⌥.
- **It does not span the window** (the 360×48 toolbar group): the container itself, as today.

**R129. What a shape says.**
- **The readout** names the members: `cluster · Recent, Currents`. At hover, the members are the non-container nodes the hover snapshot already holds that lie at least half inside the shape: the element, its `siblings`, and its `children` (R128 reads both under any parent). Each is named by its label, else its identifier, in reading order, joined by `, `; nodes with neither are left out. A long list is cut in the middle by the label, as today. With no names, the readout is `cluster`. The fallback cluster reads the same way, instead of `cluster · 2 elements`.
- **Members on commit.** When Point commits a shape (a click, Return, or a Shift-click into a set), its `members` come from the drawn-frame walk over its frame (`AccessibilityReader.elements(in:pid:)`, then `RegionResolver.elements`): every non-container element at least half inside, in reading order, at most 12. So the payload can list more members than the readout named. If the walk finds none, the members known at hover are kept. They run after the crop has started, so the crop never waits for them (`specs/capture-recovery.md` R110). The frame is the one that was outlined. Snap, Text, and Cut write no members, as today.
- **The Markdown head line** for a `LocantShape` reads `cluster · N elements (shape found on screen by Locant, not an accessibility element; frame from its visible edges)`. A `LocantCluster` keeps today's line, which ends `frame approximate`.
- **The crop** is the shape's frame plus the usual 40 pt (v0.1 R4).
- **The schema** gains no field. The `role` description in `schema/capture.schema.json` names both computed kinds: `LocantShape`, found from the visible edges, and `LocantCluster`, computed from neighboring elements.

**R132. A row of controls is a rung.** On Oct 26 Malik pointed at the toolbar capsule on Oryne's thought-detail screen in Device Hub (capture 20261026-190816-8ngf) and could not take its three buttons together with the pointer or with ⌥, only by dragging a frame or Shift-clicking; ⌥ went to the whole screen. The buttons (`folder` at x=559.6, `square.and.arrow.up` at 599, `trash` at 638.3; each 39.4×39.3 at y=398.2) touch and tile the glass capsule. So R125 step 3 finds no rim to point into, the pixel ring around a button lies mostly outside the capsule (R127 finds no shape), and the cluster (R128) pads by 16 pt and groups at 24 pt, so it takes the meta line 23 pt below the capsule with it, or misses.

- **The group.** Among the hovered element's siblings (`ElementSnapshot.siblings`, read under any container since R128), the non-container controls that, with the element, form a chain along one axis. The chain runs outward from the element in both directions, and every member has a non-empty frame.
  - **Row:** each next control's left edge lies between an overlap of 2 pt and a gap of `min(10, max(2, 0.25 × min(w, h)))` pt past where the chain ends, w and h being the element's (9.8 pt for 39.3 pt buttons, 2 pt for controls 8 pt tall, 10 pt for 75.9 pt cards). Every member's top and height match the element's within 1 pt.
  - **Column:** the same along y, with every member's left edge and width matching the element's within 1 pt.
  - **Ties:** when two controls could join at one step, the nearer joins (the smaller gap or overlap), then the first in sibling order. The chain's ends only move outward.
  - A group has at least 2 members, the element included. A row if there are two or more, else a column; else none.
- **Amended Oct 26: small gaps.** Malik's in-app check on Oryne's Library screen in Device Hub: Recent (567.1,220.0 39.4×39.3, `square.grid.2x2`) and Currents (606.5, `water.waves`) touch, and Filter (an `AXPopUpButton` at 653.9, `library.filter`) sits 8 pt past Currents, behind a divider drawn in the same glass capsule. Under the first rule (within 2 pt) ⌥ took Recent and Currents only. Now it takes the three: 567.1,220.0 126.2×39.3.
- **Capped at 10 pt Oct 27, Malik.** A quarter of a card's side reaches far: 19 pt for 75.9 pt cards, so cards 12 pt apart, ordinary list spacing, would join a column. The reach stops at 10 pt (`HitRefiner.groupGapCap`): the Library capsule's 8 pt still joins, and 12 pt between cards does not.
- **The rung.** `role` `cluster`, `rawRole` `LocantGroup`, `label`, `identifier`, and `value` null, `identifierSource` `unknown`. `frame`: the exact union of the members, with no padding. `members`: those controls in reading order. `path`: the ancestors' path, then `cluster`, as for any cluster.
- **Where it sits.**
  - **Today's ladder:** right after the element, before the sibling cluster (R128). It needs no screen sample and no permission.
  - **The shape-aware ladder (R125):** an accessibility-side rung. The cluster rule drops only a `LocantCluster`, so the group stays where a shape is found. It must hold rung 0 like any rung, takes its place by area, and a shape within 2 pt of it merges into it with the group kept. The shape would outline the same rectangle, so it adds nothing.
  - **Same frame as an accessibility rung** (`collapsingSameFrames`, unchanged): the one with an identifier stays, else the innermost, the group.
- **No seam zone.** Hover on a button stays on that button. Only ⌥ reaches the group.
- **What it says.** The readout names the members as for any cluster: `cluster · folder, square.and.arrow.up, trash` (R129, code unchanged). The Markdown head line reads `cluster · N elements (controls in one row or column, grouped by Locant, not an accessibility element; frame is their union)`; the `LocantShape` and `LocantCluster` lines are unchanged. The `role` description in `schema/capture.schema.json` gains: "rawRole LocantGroup: controls in one row or column lined up with small gaps, its frame their union." No field is added.
- **Members on commit.** The members found at hover stand: the drawn-frame walk of R129 applies only to a `LocantShape`.

**R133. Device Hub's tree.** Malik's in-app check on Oct 26 found faults only inside the simulated screen: ⌥ from a button in Oryne's Library toolbar capsule still went to the whole screen, and the pointer on the floating tab bar outlined the card behind it. The probe (Library screen, Oct 26): the screen is an `AXGroup`, subrole `iOSContentGroup`, at 347.9,137.5 359.7×780.9; its elements sit in an inner group of the same frame but name the screen as their `AXParent`; the "Tab Bar" group (347.9,844.3 359.7×74.2) has no children, and a hit test on it at 527,880 answers with the card behind it (362.2,830.9 331.1×75.9).

- **The parent that lists the element.** On the simulated screen (the element or one of the parents just read has subrole `iOSContentGroup`, whatever its frame: `HitRefiner.isOnSimulatedScreen`, the one test for what R133–R135 change only there), when the hovered element is not a container and its reported parent is a container that does not list it among its children, the reader searches below that parent for the container that does: depth first, through containers only, into nodes whose frame holds the element's within 1 pt, at most 4 levels down and 200 nodes. The parent's children are read once, through the neighbor cache, for this check (all of them) and for the siblings (the first 60, R128's cap). The answer is kept per element and reported parent for 400 ms, so an element whose reported parent changes is asked afresh, and no container joins the path twice.
  - **Found:** the siblings (R128) are that container's children, and it joins the path as the nearest ancestor, ahead of the reported parent (still at most 6 ancestors). So R132's group and R128's cluster form on the Library screen.
  - **Not found,** and off the simulated screen, where nothing is searched: the siblings come from the reported parent, as before.
  - A container under the pointer reads its own children, which need no repair.
- **The neighbor cache** is keyed by the container itself as well as its app, role, subrole, and frame, so the screen's group and the inner group, of one role and frame, never share an entry.
- **A detached screen group.** Device Hub sometimes hands back a detached copy of the screen group as the parent (an empty frame, no parent or window; seen Oct 26 after switching devices), whose frame is then the union of its children's non-empty frames. The ancestors above it stay absent.
- **Floating bars.** When the answer lies on the simulated screen (it or an ancestor has subrole `iOSContentGroup`), the screen's direct children are checked as the neighbor cache has them, elements and attributes read together at most 400 ms before: whether one of them could answer at the point, and which holds the answer. Nothing more is read for it, so the pointer in the content group, the common case, costs nothing, and only the child that answers is refined. If the answer sits in (or is) a child that spans the screen, and the point lies in another direct child with a non-empty frame that does not span it, that child answers instead, refined as a hit is (R2). Of several, the smallest answers. Where the child that holds the answer cannot be told (a parent link that skips one), the point alone decides.
  - **Spans the screen:** its frame matches the screen's within 1 pt.
  - **Why:** a screen-sized content group lies under the chrome the simulator draws over it (the tab bar, toolbars, a sheet's grabber), whatever the reading order.
  - **Today** the tab bar has no children, so the pointer on it outlines the `Tab Bar` group, with the ladder of any container answer (R128).
  - Off the simulated screen, nothing more is read.
- **Frames that hang over smaller siblings.** On the simulated screen, when the answer is not a container and one or more of its siblings (from the listing container) are non-container elements with non-empty frames that hold the point and are smaller in area than the answer, the smallest of them answers instead, read from the neighbor cache's element for it. Seen Oct 26 on Oryne's Tide screen: at 399,636, on the "Nearby" chip (362.2,616.8 74.0×39.3), the hit test answered with an image (362.2,423.5 331.1×215.0) whose frame hangs about 22 pt past the drawn picture, over "Nearby" and "Let it rest" (443.3,616.8 88.0×39.3). It costs one more snapshot, of that sibling (its attributes, its parent chain, and the check for the container that lists it), only when it applies; off the simulated screen it is not asked.
- **Unchanged:** R132's limits and R128's thresholds. Outside Device Hub, only the cache key changes, and it changes no answer.
